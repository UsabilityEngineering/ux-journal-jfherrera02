# Booking a Flight
By Jose Herrera

Recently, my friends and I started making plans to go on a trip to Japan, so I started to look for flights through my credit card's travel portal. The end goal was to find the best flights available for us at the cheapest price possible as we are university students trying to save money. 
When I was first presented with the menu, I was pleased at how straightforward it was. All I had to do was select the dates, locations, and the type of flight in order to begin my search. 

![Pic_1](blob/main/assets/booking_1.png "Pic 1")

I was then presented with a incredibly **useful** feature that presented the dates in a color coded format showcasing the varying prices of the flight to my destination. This was not something I would typically see in many other booking websites so I was very happy to see this feature.

![Pic_2](assets/booking_2.png "Pic 2")

After clicking search on my desired trip, I found myself presented with lots of information that I found to be a bit overwhelming at first, but after carefully looking into it I thought that what was presented to me was quite **valuable**. I was given lots of information on my flight in ways that I could potentially save money or have more control with booking my flight. This information presented to me was very **memorable** to me as I had never seen such depth of information and control provided anywhere else.

![Pic_3](assets/booking_3.png "Pic 3")

Finally I was given the option to choose my airline, what kind of class I would like to ride in, and all the information necessary for that specific flight. This entire booking process applies **flexibility and efficiency of use** because I was able to quickly, and without many issues, search for the flight I wanted, and I was even given several options to alter my flight in order to save money or have a better experience.
